> Baguettes are classic French breads that are very long and thin. Because Airbus A340-600s have such long bodies and elegant shapes, they look just like flying **Baguettes**. As they soar through the sky, their shiny metal bodies glimmer just like **Silver Baguettes**.

Four-engine jets have their own charm, and the Airbus A340-600 was a great example of that. Nicknamed the "flying pencil" because of its long, thin shape, the A346 was such a special model. With Lufthansa retiring this jet, looking back at some memories feels like a fitting way to say goodbye.

## The Special Lower Deck Lavatories

Even if you are not an aviation geek, you might remember this type for its lower-deck lavatories---economy class passengers had to head downstairs to use the facilities. Lufthansa chose to place five lavatories down in the lower deck to maximize high-density economy seating. Aside from a few charter A330s decades ago and vintage 1970s trijets with lower-deck galleys or lounges, this made the A340-600 practically the only modern scheduled airliner where passengers could walk downstairs mid-flight.

## The Cockpit View

Before saying goodbye, I had the chance to visit the flight deck. Stepping into the A346 cockpit was like stepping into a transitional era of aviation history, where modern glass displays met classic analogue charm, complete with the increasingly rare sight of four engine controls. It was an absolute honour to sit in the captain's seat and take in the view one last time.
